Platform: TryHackMe
Room: Operation Slither

This challenge simulated a data breach scenario in which attackers publicly announced the sale of a company's database (TryTelecomMe).
The objective was to identify the threat actors involved by analyzing publicly available information and correlating digital footprints across multiple platforms.
The investigation started with the following public message:

"
Full user database TryTelecomMe on sale!!!
As part of Operation Slither, we've been hiding for weeks in 
their network and have now started to exfiltrate information. 
This is just the beginning.
We'll be releasing more data soon. Stay tuned!
@v3n0mbyt3_
---
"
While reviewing the activity of v3n0mbyt3_, i find him on Threads, where I noticed a reply directed to a single account, _myst1cv1x3n_.
That account posted an encoded message.
The format strongly suggested Base64 encoding. 
Decoding it, the first flag appers.

Following to the next message, the second part of the lab:

"
60GB of data owned by TryTelecomMe is now up for bidding!
Number of users: 64500000 Accepting all types of crypto
For takers, send your bid on Threads via this handle:
HIDDEN CONTENT 
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

You must register or log in to view this content
"

Identify that this account have an Instagram.
Analysing it posts, we can find a link to soundcloud.
At the second track of it soundcloud account, have another Base64 enconding.
Deconding it, the second flag appers.

The third and last massage:

"
FOR SALE
Advanced automation scripts for phishing and initial access!
Inclusions:- Terraform scripts for a resilient phishing infrastructure 
- Updated Google Phishlet (evilginx v3.0)- GoPhish automation scripts
- Google MFA bypass script- Google account enumerator
- Automated Google brute-forcing script
- Cobalt Strike aggressor scripts
- SentinelOne, CrowdStrike, Cortex XDR bypass payloads
PRICE: $1500
Accepting all types of crypto
Contact me on REDACTED@protonmail.com 
--
"

A third person comes out.
Looking at the earlier SoundCloud, we found out an account who interacts a lot with that track, the sh4d0wF4NG.
After searching the alias sh4d0wF4NG, I located a GitHub profile.
After that, explore the repositories and commit history was the only way.
Analysing everything, a deleted file appers at the history commit from another file.
Within the deleted file, another Base64 string was discovered.
Decoded, it brings the final flag.
